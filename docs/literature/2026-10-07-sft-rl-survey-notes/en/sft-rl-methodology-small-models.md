# SFT + RL (GRPO-style RLVR) methodology for small (1–7B) language models: distillation-SFT vs self-taught SFT vs no SFT, and unified/interleaved schemes

Scope note: 2025-01 to 2026-10, methodology papers only (no text-to-SQL, no user-simulator agents). Numbers below were read from arXiv abstract/HTML pages via a fetch-and-extract pass; where a table value looked internally odd it is flagged. "not stated" means the page did not give the value. Each paper is tagged with its first-arXiv month.

Quick orientation (what the evidence says, one paragraph): For 1–4B policies the single most robust finding across math, tool-use and agentic settings is that *what* you SFT on matters more than *whether* you SFT. Distilled long-CoT from a much stronger teacher reliably hurts ≤3B students (Li et al. Feb 2025; BREAD Jun 2025; SFT-or-RL Apr 2025; Tool-N1 Apr 2025; ToolRL Apr 2025) because the student cannot express the teacher's trajectories, learns "pseudo-reasoning" surface patterns, and loses entropy, which then starves GRPO of within-group variance. Fixes that work for small models: mix short/long or small-teacher/large-teacher data (Li et al.), prune or truncate teacher chains (P-ALIGN Jan 2026; Efficient Long CoT May 2025), use the model's own verified rollouts or a same-family teacher as the SFT source (WinDOM Jun 2026; RLoop Nov 2025; SDFT Jan 2026), stop the SFT early / under-saturate it (WinDOM), or keep an expert signal inside RL only for prompts the policy cannot solve (LUFFY, ReLIFT, BREAD, Scaf-GRPO, Guide, CHORD). A properly implemented SFT→RL baseline still beats every published mixed-policy method on 7–8B math (Apr 2026), so the "unified" schemes are not a free win; their value is for weak/small policies where on-policy RL collapses to zero reward (LUFFY Llama-3.1-8B hard set, BREAD 1.5B/3B, DGPO 0.5B). The Qwen3 small models (0.6B–30B-A3B) were themselves produced by off-policy then on-policy distillation from Qwen3-32B/235B, not by the 4-stage RL pipeline, and Qwen reports on-policy distillation beating RL on Qwen3-8B (74.4 vs 67.6 AIME'24) at ~1/10 the GPU hours.

## KQ1. "Small models struggle to learn from strong reasoners" (Li et al. 2025) and follow-ups: when does distilled SFT hurt small models, and what fixes were proposed?

### Takeaway
Distilled long-CoT / large-teacher SFT measurably hurts ≤3B students (e.g., Qwen2.5-1.5B loses 10+ points on MATH and AMC with long CoT vs short CoT; Qwen2.5-0.5B loses >10 points on AMC when the teacher is 72B instead of 3B), and the gap closes around 3B–7B. Fixes with reported gains: mixing long:short or large:small teacher data 1:4 (+7–8 points on Qwen2.5-3B), pruning/truncating teacher chains, aligning teacher samples to student capacity (temperature schedule, divergence-aware sampling, mixed on/off-policy), and replacing teacher traces with the student's own verified rollouts.

### Cited Findings
- Li et al., "Small Models Struggle to Learn from Strong Reasoners" (Feb 2025, v3 Nov 2025). Students: Qwen2.5 0.5B/1.5B/3B/7B/14B/32B and Llama-3.2-1B/3B, 3.1-8B, 3.3-70B. Teachers: QwQ-32B-Preview (long CoT) vs Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct (short CoT); Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct vs Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct. Core claim: "small models (≤3B parameters) do not consistently benefit from long chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning or distillation from larger models." — [arXiv 2502.12143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12143)
- Same paper, numbers: Qwen2.5-1.5B shows a 10+ point deficit on MATH and AMC when trained on long CoT vs short CoT; Qwen2.5-3B shows a 3.1-point average gap favouring short CoT. Large-teacher gap: Qwen2.5-0.5B degrades "more than 10 points" on AMC with the 72B teacher vs the 3B teacher; for Qwen2.5-3B the difference is negligible (0.3 points). — [arXiv HTML 2502.12143](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12143)
- Same paper, fix: "Mix Distillation" with long:short CoT in a 1:4 ratio (Mix-Long) or large:small teacher 1:4 (Mix-Large). On Qwen2.5-3B: Mix-Long gives "more than 8 points on MATH and AMC"; Mix-Large "more than 7 points on MATH, AIME and AMC" over training on either data alone. Stated mechanisms: "distribution mismatch between student and teacher", a "speaking styles shift" (tokens like "wait", "But", "Let" over-represented in long CoT), and domain knowledge matters ("math expert models exhibit a smaller learnability gap" than general models). — [arXiv HTML 2502.12143](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12143)
- BREAD (Jun 2025), students Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, experts Gemini Thinking Experimental and DeepSeek-R1. Theoretical result: standard SFT + GRPO "can fail completely when (1) the expert's traces are too difficult for the small model to express, or (2) the small model's initialization has exponentially small likelihood of success"; formally, SFT and GRPO have "no impact on the student model with probability at least 2e^(-K²/4L)". Empirically on NuminaMath-CoT with Qwen-3B: vanilla GRPO ~46–48%, SFT+GRPO ~54%, BREAD ~65%, DeepSeek-R1-Distill reference ~66% but with 800k samples; BREAD needs "<40% of ground-truth traces" and trains ~3x faster than GRPO. — [arXiv 2506.17211](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17211), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17211)
- "SFT or RL? An Early Investigation into Training R1-Like Reasoning LVLMs" (Apr 2025), Qwen2-VL-2B/7B and Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B: SFT on distilled reasoning paths before GRPO caused an average 12.7% drop vs RL without SFT; SFT-only on the 3B model caused a "47% relative performance drop" vs base. Mechanism: SFT induces "pseudo reasoning paths" and "pseudo aha moments" — imitation of structure without capability; direct GRPO (VLAA-Thinker-Qwen2.5-VL-3B) topped the Open LMM Reasoning Leaderboard at 4B scale. (Multimodal, but the 2–3B scale is the point.) — [arXiv HTML 2504.11468](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11468v1)
- Nemotron-Research-Tool-N1 (Apr 2025), tool calling, Qwen2.5-7B/14B-Instruct primary with experiments down to Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B/3B. With 5,518 DeepSeek-R1-distilled trajectories: Pure RL 83.24% BFCL avg vs Reason-SFT 82.71% vs SFT-then-RL 83.17%; "the widely adopted SFT-then-RL paradigm does not necessarily outperform pure RL"; distilled reasoning annotations add little in SFT (No-Reason SFT 81.47% vs Reason-SFT 82.71%); SFT on distilled traces gives "imitative reasoning that limits generalization". — [arXiv 2505.00024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00024), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00024)
- LS-Mixture SFT (May 2025): mixes long CoT with structure-preserved short rewrites; accuracy +2.3% average and response length −47.61%; motivation: students "inherit the 'overthinking' problem from teacher models". Model sizes not stated on the abstract page. — [arXiv 2505.03469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03469)
- "Efficient Long CoT Reasoning in Small Language Models" (May 2025): direct long-CoT distillation into SLMs is ineffective because "the long CoT often contains a lot of redundant contents (e.g., overthinking steps) which may make SLMs hard to learn"; fix = prune redundant steps, then an on-policy stage where the SLM curates its own data. Sizes/numbers not stated on abstract page. — [arXiv 2505.18440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18440)
- P-ALIGN, "Long-Chain Reasoning Distillation via Adaptive Prefix Alignment" (Jan 2026): teacher chains "often excessively long and structurally complex, making them difficult for student models to learn"; adaptively truncates to a prefix and supervises on that; >3% over baselines on math. Sizes not stated. — [arXiv 2601.10064](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10064)
- Distribution-Aligned Sequence Distillation (Jan 2026), student Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, teacher gpt-oss-120b: three failure modes of SFT distillation — poor coverage of teacher's sequence distribution, "misalignment between the teacher's output distribution and the student's learning capacity", and exposure bias; fixes = temperature-scheduled samples (T=0.6 then 1.0), divergence-aware sampling (teacher-confident/student-unlikely examples), and mixed on/off-policy data. Result on 448K samples: AIME25 83.3% (vs 81.3% Qwen3-4B-Thinking), AIME24 88.5%, LiveCodeBench v5 69.3%, GPQA-Diamond 68.4%. — [arXiv HTML 2601.09088](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09088v1)
- HEAL (Mar 2026, EMNLP 2026 Findings): standard distillation has a "Teacher Ceiling" (hard problems discarded when the teacher fails); uses entropy dynamics to find reasoning breakpoints and inject hindsight hints; grounded in Zone-of-Proximal-Development; sizes/numbers not stated on abstract page. — [arXiv 2603.10359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10359)
- "RL vs Distillation: Understanding Accuracy and Capability" (May 2025): RLVR improves pass@1 but "often fails to improve capability (pass@k)"; distillation improves capability only when it introduces new knowledge, "distilling reasoning patterns only improves accuracy but not capability"; sizes not stated on abstract page. — [arXiv 2505.14216](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14216)
- DeepSeek-R1 (Jan 2025) distilled models were SFT-only ("we apply only SFT and do not include an RL stage") on ~800k samples (600k reasoning + 200k non-reasoning); DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B: 28.9% AIME24 pass@1, 83.9% MATH-500; -7B: 55.5% / 92.8%. The same report's 32B comparison: RL-from-base (DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B) 47.0% AIME24 vs Distill-Qwen-32B 72.6%, with the conclusion that smaller models "relying on the large-scale RL ... require enormous computational power and may not even achieve the performance of distillation". — [arXiv HTML 2501.12948](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12948v1)

### Inferences
- The failure mode is scale-dependent and sharp: Li et al. show the long-CoT/large-teacher penalty at 0.5B–1.5B, marginal at 3B, gone by 7B. For a Qwen3-1.7B policy, naïve distillation from a 32B/72B teacher's long trajectories is the recipe most likely to hurt; for Qwen3-4B it is roughly neutral-to-slightly-negative unless the data is length/difficulty-matched.
- Three fixes are consistent across papers: (a) make the traces expressible by the student (shorter, pruned, small-teacher-mixed, prefix-truncated); (b) let the student generate or co-generate the traces (on-policy stage, mixed-policy distillation); (c) keep the hard-problem expert signal for RL time (hints/prefixes) rather than putting it into SFT.
- DeepSeek-R1's "distillation beats RL for small models" claim is about SFT on 800k teacher samples with the RL budget held at 32B scale; it does not contradict Li et al. because R1-Distill students were ≥1.5B *math* models (Qwen2.5-Math) on which the learnability gap is smallest.

### Gaps
- No paper found that reports the Li et al. learnability gap specifically on Qwen3-1.7B/4B (all 2025 studies use Qwen2.5 or Llama-3.2 students).
- No direct measurement found of the gap in an agentic/tool-use setting at ≤2B (Tool-N1 runs 0.5B–3B but the page did not give those per-size numbers).

## KQ2. STaR / ReST-EM / RFT "self-taught" cold start vs distillation: is SFT on the model's own filtered trajectories a better RL warm start than teacher distillation?

### Takeaway
Direct 2025–2026 evidence favours self-generated or same-family data as the RL warm start for small models: WinDOM (Jun 2026, Qwen3.5-2B) shows an under-saturated rejection-sampling cold start beats a converged one by +5.4 OOD points after identical GRPO, with an EMA-of-self teacher within 1.1 points of a 4B teacher; RLoop (Nov 2025) shows RFT-from-own-RL-rollouts re-initialization gives +9% accuracy / +15% pass@32 over vanilla RL; SDFT (Jan 2026) shows on-policy self-distillation beats offline teacher distillation and improves pass@k up to k=128. The one controlled SFT-vs-on-policy-distillation warm-start study (Jun 2026, 7B VLM) finds OPD gives 5–8x higher entropy at init but the post-RL difference is within 1.1 points — so the warm-start choice sets the entropy regime more than the outcome. Caveat: self-distillation on math can suppress uncertainty verbalisation and drop OOD performance by up to 40% (Mar 2026, incl. Qwen3-1.7B).

### Cited Findings
- Background (brief): STaR (2022), ReST-EM (2023) and RFT (2023) established SFT on the model's own filtered correct rationales; the 2025 RAFT revisit found "a simple rejection sampling baseline (RAFT) that trains only on positively rewarded samples yields competitive performance compared to GRPO and PPO" with faster early convergence. — [arXiv 2504.11343 "from Rejection Sampling to Reinforce"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.11343)
- WinDOM, "Self-Family Distillation for Small-Model GUI Grounding" (Jun 2026), student Qwen3.5-2B. Cold start = one rejection-sampling SFT whose teacher is either an EMA of the student itself (no external model) or a frozen same-family 4B; rollouts kept only if the parsed click lies inside the ground-truth box. Key finding: "the same GRPO recipe yields opposite OOD outcomes under the two cold-start depths" — the under-saturated (early) cold start, after GRPO, beats the converged cold start by +3.5 pts ScreenSpot-Pro (55.7 vs 52.2), +7.0 OSWorld-G (54.5 vs 47.5), +5.8 ScreenSpot-V2 (88.8 vs 83.0), OOD-mean +5.4 (66.3 vs 60.9). EMA-of-self teacher reaches 65.2 vs 66.3 OOD-mean for the 4B teacher. Mechanism: "a saturated cold-start has been driven by cross-entropy onto a near-deterministic action distribution", so "the within-group standard deviation σ approaches zero and the group-relative advantage degenerates", leaving GRPO no signal. — [arXiv HTML 2606.25964](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25964v1)
- RLoop (Nov 2025): iterative cycles of RL exploration → filter successful trajectories → rejection-sampling fine-tuning (RFT) → new RL init; targets "RL overfitting" from "policy over-specialization and catastrophic forgetting of diverse solutions"; +9% average accuracy and +15% pass@32 vs vanilla RL. Model sizes not stated on abstract page. — [arXiv 2511.04285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04285)
- GLM-4.5 (Aug 2025) applies "iterative self-distillation to enhance SFT cold-start performance by substituting original cold-start data with responses generated by the RL-trained model" (search-snippet level; large model). — [arXiv 2508.06471](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.06471)
- SDFT, "Self-Distillation Enables Continual Learning" (Jan 2026), Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct primary, also Qwen2.5-3B/14B and Olmo-3-7B-Think. Teacher = same model conditioned on an in-context expert demonstration; student samples on-policy, reverse-KL. On a tool-use task "offline distillation from the teacher improves over standard SFT, it consistently underperforms on-policy SDFT." Claims "SDFT can serve as an effective initialization for subsequent RL fine-tuning" and "SDFT improves pass@k across a wide range of k" (k up to 128); no RL-after-SDFT benchmark numbers given. — [arXiv HTML 2601.19897](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19897v1)
- "Stage-1 Controls the Entropy Regime, Not the Outcome: On-Policy Distillation versus SFT as a Warm-Start for Small-Data VLM RL" (Jun 2026). Student Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, teacher Qwen2.5-VL-72B, 2,101 Geometry3K prompts, Stage-2 GRPO (lr 5e-7, batch 8, group 5). Init pass@16: OPD 74.4% (diversity 28.1%), SFT_OOD 72.4% (24.2%), SFT_ID (rejection-sampled teacher traces) 69.2% (21.9%). Init entropy: OPD 0.10–0.16 vs SFT 0.015–0.04 (5–8x). After GRPO pass@16: OPD→GRPO 73.4, SFT_ID→GRPO 72.4, SFT_OOD→GRPO 73.5 (all within 1.1 pts, CIs cross zero); MathVista OOD all within 1.2 pts (88.5–89.7) and the entropy ordering disappears. Authors: Stage-1 "is strongly associated with the entropy regime in this setup, but the downstream payoff is small, localized, and not evidence that OPD is a better RL warm-start." Confound: OPD saw all 2,101 problems; rejection-sampled SFT covered only 807 (1,294 hardest omitted). — [arXiv HTML 2606.09059](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09059)
- CurioSFT (Feb 2026, v3 Jul 2026), Qwen2.5-Math-7B primary plus Qwen3-4B-Base and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct: "the SFT stage locks the model into a low-diversity mode, severely constraining the search space for the subsequent RL stage." Self-exploratory distillation against a temperature-scaled self-teacher with entropy-gated per-token temperature. SFT-stage: ID 48.5→51.0, OOD 51.3→54.2, entropy 0.31→0.43 nats. After GRPO: ID +5.0 avg, AIME24 39.2% vs 32.1% for SFT→RL; OOD +2.4. — [arXiv HTML 2602.02244](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02244v3)
- TS-OPSD, "Internalize the Temperature" (May 2026, EMNLP 2026 Findings), Qwen3-4B-Base and Qwen3-8B-Base: distilling a high-temperature self-teacher back into an entropy-collapsed RL checkpoint "yields a stronger initialization for continued RL than both standard continued RL and rollout-level temperature reheating"; needs "no external teacher, privileged data, or additional inference cost"; mechanism: "mainly reduces output sharpness while preserving intermediate representations, top candidate sets, and reasoning capability." — [arXiv 2606.00755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00755)
- "Why Does Self-Distillation (Sometimes) Degrade the Reasoning Capability of LLMs?" (Mar 2026, COLM 2026), Qwen3-1.7B/8B, DeepSeek-Distill-Qwen-7B, Olmo3-7B-Instruct: self-distillation hurts math reasoning with "performance drops of up to 40%" despite shorter responses; mechanism = "suppression of epistemic verbalization" — teachers conditioned on rich info over limited task coverage stop expressing uncertainty, which helps in-domain but hurts OOD where "unseen problems benefit from expressing uncertainty and adjusting accordingly." — [arXiv 2603.24472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24472)
- RL's Razor (Sep 2025): "on-policy RL is implicitly biased towards KL-minimal solutions among the many that solve the new task, whereas SFT can converge to distributions arbitrarily far from the base model"; forgetting tracks KL(fine-tuned || base). Sizes not stated on abstract page. — [arXiv 2509.04259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04259)
- Tailor, "Tailored Primitive Initialization is the Secret Key to RL" (Nov 2025): RL outcome depends on initialization's "reasoning token coverage"; curates reasoning primitives to expand the state distribution before RL; sizes and numbers not stated on abstract page. — [arXiv 2511.12429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12429)
- Thinking Machines, "On-Policy Distillation" (Oct 2025): Qwen3-8B-Base student, Qwen3-32B teacher, AIME'24: off-policy distillation (SFT, 400k prompts) 60%; RL (17,920 GPU-h) 67.6%; on-policy distillation (1,800 GPU-h) 74.4%; 9–30x cheaper than an extrapolated SFT-2M baseline; a single prompt with 5,120 graded sequences matched the teacher; after mid-training degraded IF-eval 85→45, OPD restored it to 83 while keeping knowledge gains (36→41). Mechanism: RL "provides very sparse feedback, teaching a fixed number of bits per training episode", OPD is on-policy with dense per-token reward. — [thinkingmachines.ai](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)

### Inferences
- The mechanism that unites the pro-self-taught results is entropy/variance preservation for GRPO: cross-entropy on off-policy teacher text collapses the policy; own-sample SFT (which by construction sits near the base distribution; cf. RL's Razor) or early-stopped SFT keeps σ>0 inside rollout groups.
- The Jun 2026 controlled study is the strongest "it doesn't matter much" result: for a 7B student with a strong teacher and plenty of RL, cold-start flavour washes out. The results that *do* show a durable difference are at ≤3B or where the teacher is far off-distribution (WinDOM 2B; ToolRL 1.5B/3B; BREAD 1.5B/3B).
- Self-taught data has its own failure mode for math-style reasoning (uncertainty suppression); for tool/agent tasks with deterministic verifiers (WinDOM, SDFT tool-use) the reported effect is positive. Rejection-sampled own-trajectories are therefore safer when the verifier is exact and the task is procedural.

### Gaps
- No paper found that runs the three-way comparison (teacher-distilled SFT vs own-rejection-sampled SFT vs no SFT → same GRPO) on a 1–4B text model in math or agentic tasks with numbers; the closest are WinDOM (2B, GUI grounding), ToolRL (1.5B/3B, SFT-400 vs none), and the 7B VLM study.
- RLoop and Tailor pages did not give model sizes.

## KQ3. "SFT memorizes, RL generalizes"; "RL squeezes, SFT expands"; pass@k boundary papers; Invisible Leash; ProRL; Beyond 80/20: does SFT before RL restrict exploration, and how much SFT is "enough"?

### Takeaway
The pass@k literature says RLVR mostly re-weights what the base can already sample (base beats RL at large k; Minerva 32B ~9% at k=128), SFT/distillation is what adds new modes, and SFT→RL works because "SFT expands correct trajectories while subsequent RL compresses incorrect trajectories" (1.5B/7B/14B). ProRL (1.5B, prolonged with KL control and reference resets) is the counter-example where RL does exceed the base at all k. On "how much SFT": the Apr 2026 SFT-then-RL paper shows 3 epochs on 46k traces then only 50 RL steps already lands within 1.4 points of 500 steps (7B), while the Jan 2025 SFT-memorizes paper and SimpleRL-Zoo show SFT is needed mainly to stabilise format for RL and that zero-RL works from 0.5B upward with difficulty-matched data and a loose format reward.

### Cited Findings
- "SFT Memorizes, RL Generalizes" (Jan 2025): on GeneralPoints and V-IRL, "RL, especially when trained with an outcome-based reward, generalizes across both rule-based textual and visual variants" while SFT "tends to memorize training data"; yet "SFT remains essential for effective RL training; SFT stabilizes the model's output format, enabling subsequent RL to achieve its performance gains." Sizes not stated on abstract page (Llama-3.2-Vision-11B in the paper). — [arXiv 2501.17161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17161)
- "RL Squeezes, SFT Expands" (Sep 2025, ICLR 2026). Sizes 1.5B/7B/14B; base Qwen2.5-Math; RL-zero variants Oat-Zero, SimpleRL-Zoo; SFT variants DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen; SFT+RL variants Nemotron-Research-Reasoning / AceReason-Nemotron; AIME24/25, AMC23. "RL decreases the number of unique incorrect trajectories" while "SFT increases the number of unique correct trajectories"; RL steepens step-visitation decay ~2.5x (concentrates reasoning into few steps), SFT flattens to ~1/3. Rationale for SFT→RL: "SFT expands correct trajectories while subsequent RL compresses incorrect trajectories." 1.5B models show the same pattern as 7B/14B. — [arXiv 2509.21128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21128v1), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.21128v1)
- "Does RL Really Incentivize Reasoning Capacity Beyond the Base Model?" (Apr 2025). Math: Qwen2.5-7B/14B/32B base, LLaMA-3.1-8B; code: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B; vision: Qwen2.5-VL-7B. RL wins at small k, "base models achieve a higher pass@k score when k is large"; Minerva 32B base beats RL by ~9% at k=128; RL responses sit in the low-perplexity region of the base ("PPL_Base(Y_RL|x) closely matches the lower portion of the PPL_Base(Y_Base|x) distribution"); sampling-efficiency gap ΔSE >40 for all six RL algorithms (GRPO 43.9, RLOO 42.6). Distillation: "the pass@k curve of the distilled model is consistently and significantly above that of the base model" — "distillation can introduce new reasoning patterns from the teacher and genuinely expand the model's reasoning capabilities." — [arXiv 2504.13837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13837), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13837)
- "The Invisible Leash" (Jul 2025, final Feb 2026): RLVR is "a support-constrained optimization mechanism"; "the shrinkage of empirical support generally outweighs the expansion of empirical support under larger sampling budgets"; token-level entropy can rise while answer-level entropy falls; escaping requires "seeding probability mass into underrepresented solution regions" (i.e., an off-policy/exploration source). Sizes not stated on abstract page. — [arXiv 2507.14843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14843)
- ProRL (May 2025): Nemotron-Research-Reasoning-Qwen-1.5B (built on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B per RL-Squeezes classification above) with KL control, reference-policy resets and a diverse task suite; "prolonged RL training can uncover novel reasoning strategies that are inaccessible to base models, even under extensive sampling"; "RL-trained models consistently outperform base models across a wide range of pass@k evaluations, including scenarios where base models fail entirely." Step counts and exact gains not on abstract page. — [arXiv 2505.24864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24864)
- "Beyond the 80/20 Rule" (Jun 2025, NeurIPS 2025), Qwen3-8B/14B/32B: updating only the top-20% high-entropy "forking" tokens matches full-gradient on 8B and beats it on 14B (+4.79 AIME'25, +5.21 AIME'24) and 32B (+11.04 AIME'25, +7.71 AIME'24); training on the low-entropy 80% degrades sharply. Note: gain grows with size and is ~zero at 8B — no evidence below 8B. — [arXiv 2506.01939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01939)
- SimpleRL-Zoo (Mar 2025, COLM 2025): zero-RL (no SFT) on 10 base models incl. "all Qwen2.5 models from 0.5B to 32B", Llama3-8B, Mistral-7B/24B, DeepSeek-Math-7B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B: "substantial improvements in both reasoning accuracy and response length across most settings"; format reward matters but strict enforcement hurt smaller/weaker models; difficulty-matched queries were key; "increased response length does not always correlate with the emergence of certain cognitive behaviors such as verification." — [arXiv 2503.18892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18892)
- "SFT-then-RL Outperforms Mixed-Policy Methods" (Apr 2026), Qwen2.5-Math-7B and Llama-3.1-8B. SFT recipe: OpenR1-Math-46k-8192 (DeepSeek-R1 demos), 3 epochs, batch 64, lr 5e-5 cosine; RL 500 steps. Qwen2.5-Math-7B avg ID: SFT-only 52.2, SFT→RL 57.0 (AIME24 40.4, AIME25 29.8, AMC 73.2, MATH-500 92.0); LUFFY 50.1, ReLIFT 48.8, SRFT 53.2 (reported). Llama-3.1-8B avg: SFT-only 33.9, SFT→RL 43.7, LUFFY 14.4, ReLIFT 15.6. Truncated 50-step RL: 55.6 (1.4 behind 500 steps). Entropy under SFT→RL stays "relatively constant", "refining an already strong policy rather than exploring from scratch." Also finds two bugs that suppressed prior SFT baselines (DeepSpeed CPU-offloaded optimizer dropping micro-batches; OpenRLHF per-mini-batch loss weighting). — [arXiv 2604.23747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23747), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23747)
- "On the Non-decoupling of SFT and RL in Post-training" (Jan 2026), validated on Qwen3-0.6B: RL after SFT raises the SFT cross-entropy (KL and PL-landscape analyses); SFT after RL lowers RL reward; derives an optimal RL duration and a "non-decoupling threshold" for when RL can improve on SFT. — [arXiv 2601.07389](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2601.07389)
- "A Practical Two-Stage Recipe for Mathematical LLMs" (Jul 2025): "extending SFT for as many as 10 epochs is crucial for performance breakthroughs"; "the primary role of GRPO in this framework is to optimize solution length." Size not stated on abstract page (AIMO entry). — [arXiv 2507.08267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08267)
- Light-R1 (Mar 2025): curriculum SFT 76k then 3k on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B, then GRPO: AIME24 72.3→74.0, AIME25 58.9→60.2; authors stress RL "on already long-COT finetuned models is not yet widely reached by the community." — [arXiv HTML 2503.10460](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.10460v1)
- "SFT Doesn't Always Hurt General Capabilities" (Sep 2025, ICLR 2026): "using a smaller learning rate can substantially mitigate general performance degradation while preserving comparable target-domain performance"; proposes TALR (token-adaptive loss reweighting) which beats L2, LoRA, model averaging, FLOW. Sizes/numbers not on abstract page. — [arXiv 2509.20758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20758)

### Inferences
- The two camps reconcile at the level of mechanism: RL sharpens within the base's support (pass@k, Leash, Squeezes), SFT/distillation moves the support (Squeezes, pass@k distillation curves). Hence SFT restricts exploration only when it collapses entropy (CurioSFT 0.31 nats; WinDOM σ→0; LUFFY on-policy entropy →0 after ~200 steps) — the restriction is a property of the SFT *depth/data*, not of SFT per se; a short, in-support SFT leaves plenty of exploration (Apr 2026: entropy flat under SFT→RL and RL still adds +4.8).
- "Enough SFT" has two readings in the evidence: (i) for weak/small policies, enough to get non-zero group variance on the target format (SFT-memorizes; SimpleRL-Zoo's format reward; EnvFactory's 1.6k below); (ii) for strong 7B math models with abundant verified traces, more SFT (3–10 epochs) is better and RL then needs very few steps. For a 1.7B/4B policy the Li et al./BREAD results argue for reading (i) with student-expressible data.
- Beyond-80/20's size trend (zero gain at 8B) warns against importing 14B/32B-derived RL tricks to 1.7B/4B without re-measuring.

### Gaps
- No paper quantifies an SFT-size sweep (e.g., 200 / 1k / 5k examples) against downstream GRPO on a 1–4B text model; ToolRL's single "SFT-400" point (KQ6) is the closest.
- ProRL exact numbers (steps, deltas) and Invisible Leash model sizes were not on the abstract pages.

## KQ4. Unified / interleaved SFT+RL: SRFT, LUFFY, ReLIFT, CHORD, SASR, BREAD, TAPO/TemplateRL, BRIDGE, Scaf-GRPO, Guide/hint GRPO, on-policy distillation — recipes, numbers, model sizes

### Takeaway
All mixed-policy methods report +3 to +13 points over GRPO on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (LUFFY +6.4 avg; SRFT 59.1 vs SFT→RL 54.5; ReLIFT 52.6 with 13% of the demos; CHORD-φ 62.5 vs 58.4 SFT→RL on AMC; Scaf-GRPO AIME24 30.0→43.3; TemplateRL +99% AIME), but a properly implemented SFT→RL baseline (Apr 2026) beats all of them (57.0 vs best 53.2 on 7B; 43.7 vs ~15 on Llama-3.1-8B). Their robust value is on weak/small policies where on-policy GRPO collapses to zero reward: LUFFY trains Llama-3.1-8B on the hard set where GRPO reward hits zero; BREAD lifts Qwen2.5-3B NuminaMath from ~54 (SFT+GRPO) to ~65; Scaf-GRPO lifts Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B AIME24 13.3→20.0; DGPO lifts a 0.5B agentic-RAG student from 0.006 to 0.329 (PPO 0.238, KD 0.298). On-policy distillation (Qwen3 report / Thinking Machines) is the strongest "unified" result: Qwen3-8B 74.4 vs 67.6 AIME'24 at 1,800 vs 17,920 GPU-h.

### Cited Findings
- LUFFY (Apr 2025). Qwen2.5-Math-7B primary; also Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, Qwen2.5-Instruct-7B, LLaMA-3.1-8B. Off-policy data: DeepSeek-R1 traces from OpenR1-Math-220k, 45k prompts, 1 off-policy + 7 on-policy rollouts per prompt, mixed-policy GRPO with "policy shaping via regularized importance sampling to avoid superficial and rigid imitation." 7B: AIME24/25 avg 26.3 vs SFT 22.3 / SFT+RL 24.5 / Simple-RL 16.9 / Oat-Zero 22.7; AMC 65.6 vs 52.8/62.7; MATH-500 87.6 vs 82.6/87.2; Minerva 37.5 vs 40.8/39.7; Olympiad 57.2 vs 43.7/50.4; "+6.4 points on average"; OOD +6.2 (ARC-c, GPQA-d, MMLU-Pro). Entropy: "generation entropy of On-Policy RL rapidly converges to nearly zero after approximately 200 steps"; LUFFY "consistently sustains higher entropy." Weak model: for LLaMA-3.1-8B "on-policy reinforcement learning performs well on the Easy subset but fails on the Hard subset, where training rewards collapse to zero," LUFFY "achieves stable reward improvements on both." SFT models show "redundant and circular reasoning with excessive repetition." — [arXiv 2504.14945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14945), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14945)
- SRFT (Jun 2025). Qwen2.5-Math-7B, OpenR1-Math-46k-8192. Single stage, entropy-aware weighting of SFT and RL losses. Five-benchmark avg 59.1 (AIME24 35.3, AMC 74.3, MATH500 89.8, Minerva 39.7, Olympiad 58.3) vs SFT 54.3, LUFFY 55.5, TAPO 55.7, ReLIFT 54.0, SFT→RL 54.5; +9.0 over zero-RL; OOD 62.5 (ARC-C 85.3, GPQA-D 46.4, MMLU-Pro 55.9), +4.7 over best baseline. Mechanism: "SFT induces coarse-grained global changes to LLM policy distributions, while RL performs fine-grained selective optimizations, with entropy serving as a critical indicator"; sequential SFT→RL shows rapid entropy collapse after RL begins. — [arXiv 2506.19767](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19767), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19767)
- ReLIFT (Jun 2025, ICLR 2026). Qwen2.5-Math-7B primary; Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B for generalisation. RL by default; collect high-quality solutions for questions with accuracy 0 and fine-tune when enough accumulate. Uses 8,640 demos (vs 46k for SFT baselines, "13%"). 7B overall: ReLIFT 52.6, SFT 49.0, RL 46.9, LUFFY 50.9, SFT→RL 50.8; "+5.2 points" over RL across 5 competition + 1 OOD benchmark. Mechanism: "RL excels at improving performance on questions within the model's original capabilities, while SFT is more effective at enabling progress on questions beyond the current scope." — [arXiv 2506.07527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07527), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07527)
- CHORD (Aug 2025, ICLR 2026). Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (math), Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (validation), LLaMA3.2-3B-Instruct (tool use, ToolAce 500 SFT / 5k RL, eval BFCL). Math: OpenR1-Math-220k, 5k SFT + 20k RL samples; experts = DeepSeek-R1 chosen to be off-distribution to test "shift-readapt-overfit". SFT as an auxiliary loss inside GRPO with global μ decaying 0.9→0.05 over the first 200 steps and token weight φ=p_t(1−p_t) that down-weights probability extremes "to prevent both entropy collapse and policy disruption." AMC: original 43.8, SFT-best+RL 58.4, GRPO 52.1, LUFFY 52.8, CHORD-μ 60.8, CHORD-φ 62.5. Code in modelscope/Trinity-RFT. — [arXiv 2508.11408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11408), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11408)
- SASR (May 2025): "SFT for initial warm-up to establish basic reasoning skills," then an adaptive mix of SFT and GRPO steps driven by gradient norm and KL to the original distribution; "outperforms SFT, RL, and static hybrid training methods"; sizes/numbers not on abstract page. — [arXiv 2505.13026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13026)
- BREAD (Jun 2025): see KQ1 — Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B-Instruct; branched rollouts from expert prefixes when self-generated traces fail; NuminaMath-CoT 3B: GRPO ~46–48, SFT+GRPO ~54, BREAD ~65; <40% of traces, ~3x faster. — [arXiv HTML 2506.17211](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.17211)

- HiLL, "Learning to Hint for RL" (Apr 2026): jointly trains a hinter policy and a reasoner; hints generated online conditioned on the reasoner's wrong rollout to break advantage collapse (search-snippet level; numbers not read). — [arXiv 2604.00698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00698)
- DGPO, "Can Compact Language Models Search Like Agents?" (Aug 2025, v4 Apr 2026). Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct student with Qwen2.5-3B teacher (also 7B→0.5B, Llama-3.2-1B). Problem: compact models have "poor initial performance, resulting in sparse rewards and unstable training." Recipe: KD cold start on teacher outputs, then RL where the reference model is "an active guidance mechanism" — reward for correct self-reasoning, teacher-KL pull when wrong. Avg over datasets (3B→0.5B): student 0.006, PPO 0.238, KD 0.298, DGPO 0.329, teacher 0.353. — [arXiv HTML 2508.20324](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20324)
- Qwen3 Technical Report (May 2025), strong-to-weak distillation for Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B/4B/8B/14B/30B-A3B: off-policy distillation on teacher outputs from both /think and /no_think, then on-policy distillation where the student samples and "is then fine-tuned by aligning its logits with those of a teacher model (Qwen3-32B or Qwen3-235B-A22B)." Qwen3-8B table: off-policy distillation AIME'24 55.0 / AIME'25 42.8 / pass@64 90.0; + RL 67.6 / 55.5 / 90.0 at 17,920 GPU-h; + on-policy distillation 74.4 / 65.5 / 93.3 at 1,800 GPU-h ("approximately only 1/10 of the GPU hours"). — [arXiv HTML 2505.09388](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09388)
- Thinking Machines (Oct 2025) independently reports the same Qwen3-8B numbers (60 → 67.6 → 74.4) and frames OPD as on-policy sampling + dense reverse-KL reward. — [thinkingmachines.ai](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/on-policy-distillation/)
- SOD, "Step-wise On-policy Distillation for Small LM Agents" (May 2026): students Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B, teacher Qwen3-4B (14B for scaling), tool-integrated multi-step reasoning on AIME24/25, GPQA-Diamond, LiveCodeBench; baselines SFT, GRPO, standard OPD, self-distillation (with GT / hints). Failure mode: after a wrong tool call "subsequent teacher supervision becomes unreliable," so SOD reweights distillation per step by student–teacher divergence. Up to 20.86% relative gain over next-best; 0.6B reaches 26.13% avg on AIME25. — [arXiv HTML 2605.07725](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07725v1)
- SEED (Jul 2026): self-evolving on-policy distillation for agentic RL — policy writes natural-language skills from its own trajectories, re-scores actions with/without the skill context, uses the shift as dense token-level signal; sizes/numbers not on abstract page. — [arXiv 2607.14777](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.14777)
- PACT (Jun 2026): "trace-conditioned RL surrogate that evaluates prompt-only rollouts under expert-trace context" plus component-aware SFT loss, expert traces used only during training; FTRL, BFCL, ToolHop; sizes/numbers not on abstract page. — [arXiv 2606.16215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16215)
- "SFT-then-RL Outperforms Mixed-Policy Methods" (Apr 2026): see KQ3 — with correct SFT, two-stage beats LUFFY/ReLIFT/SRFT by +3.8 (Qwen2.5-Math-7B) and +22.2 (Llama-3.1-8B) points; attributes prior mixed-policy wins to two implementation bugs suppressing the SFT baseline. — [arXiv 2604.23747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23747)

### Inferences
- Mixed-policy methods share one real mechanism: deliver an expert signal only where GRPO's advantage is zero (unsolved prompts), as a hint/prefix/off-policy sample with importance correction, while keeping most gradient on-policy to preserve entropy. On a 1.7B/4B policy facing a non-trivial fraction of zero-success tasks this is the case where they earn their complexity; where the policy already solves a reasonable share of prompts, the Apr 2026 result says a clean SFT→RL is at least as good and simpler.
- On-policy distillation is the only "unified" recipe with a large-model-lab-scale validation on Qwen3 small models (it is literally how Qwen3-1.7B/4B were made) and the only one with an order-of-magnitude compute claim; it requires a teacher that is good at the exact task and tokenizer-compatible (same family).

### Gaps
- Mixed-policy numbers at 1.5B exist only as "also works" statements (LUFFY, ReLIFT, Scaf-GRPO 1.5B AIME 13.3→20.0, CHORD 1.5B validation); none of them tabulates 1.5B against a tuned SFT→RL baseline.
- SASR, BRIDGE, TemplateRL, SEED, PACT, HiLL pages did not give model sizes.
- The Apr 2026 re-evaluation did not include CHORD, BREAD or Scaf-GRPO in the pages read.

## KQ5. Qwen3 technical report: how were Qwen3-1.7B/4B post-trained, and what does that imply for further fine-tuning? Thinking vs non-thinking under RL for small models

### Takeaway
Qwen3-1.7B/4B (and 0.6B/8B/14B/30B-A3B) were not put through the 4-stage pipeline; they were produced by "strong-to-weak distillation": off-policy SFT on Qwen3-32B/235B outputs in both /think and /no_think modes, then on-policy distillation (student samples, logits aligned to the teacher). The flagship pipeline used a "carefully selected subset" of verified long-CoT for cold start, then ~4k hard math/code problems for GRPO, then thinking-mode fusion and general RL. Implication: the released 1.7B/4B checkpoints already carry a teacher's distribution for both modes and a built-in thinking budget; further SFT on another teacher's long CoT is the exact scenario Li et al. warn about. Direct evidence on thinking vs non-thinking under RL for small Qwen3 is thin (search-snippet level only).

### Cited Findings
- Qwen3 report (May 2025): 4-stage pipeline — "(1) long chain-of-thought (CoT) cold start, (2) reasoning-based reinforcement learning (RL), (3) thinking mode fusion, and (4) general RL." Cold-start data spans math, code, logic and STEM with "verified reference answers or code-based test cases"; query filtering drops problems answerable without reasoning; response filtering removes wrong answers, repetition, inconsistencies; training uses "a carefully selected subset of the refined dataset" with minimal samples so as not to limit the model's potential. — [arXiv HTML 2505.09388](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09388)
- Qwen3 report: Reasoning RL used GRPO on approximately 4,000 hard math/code query-verifier pairs (search-snippet phrasing: "approximately 4,000 hard math/code problems"). — [arXiv PDF 2505.09388](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.09388)
- Qwen3 report: "Strong-to-Weak Distillation" targets Qwen3-0.6B, 1.7B, 4B, 8B, 14B, 30B-A3B; off-policy phase "combines teacher outputs from /think and /no_think modes"; on-policy phase: student generates, then "is fine-tuned by aligning its logits with those of a teacher model (Qwen3-32B or Qwen3-235B-A22B)." Qwen3-8B: off-policy distill 55.0/42.8 (AIME'24/'25), +RL 67.6/55.5 (17,920 GPU-h), +on-policy distill 74.4/65.5 (1,800 GPU-h); pass@64 AIME'24 90.0 → 90.0 → 93.3. Thinking budget emerges from thinking-mode fusion without explicit training ("manually halt the thinking process and insert the stop-thinking instruction"). — [arXiv HTML 2505.09388](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09388)
- Qwen3 blog (Apr 2025): post-training "empowers both thinking (reasoning) and non-thinking modes" with seamless switching in one model. — [qwenlm.github.io](https://qwenlm.github.io/blog/qwen3/)
- Entropy dynamics differ by base family: in Qwen3 experiments "GRPO experienced early entropy collapse and underperformed … compared to other RL methods," whereas on Qwen2.5 "LOOP/GRPO do not experience rapid entropy collapse … whereas RLOO does," suggesting "base-model characteristics play a major role in entropy dynamics during training irrespective of the RL algorithm" (search-snippet; paper not read in full). — [arXiv 2606.00135](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.00135)
- GEPO (Aug 2025, search-snippet): "In non-think mode, all methods struggle with exploration" on Qwen3-class models; "non-think mode limits reasoning." — [arXiv 2508.17850](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.17850)
- A controlled RLVR data-allocation study used "Qwen3-4B non-thinking RL with GRPO … rollout n=8 and train batch size 128" (search-snippet). — [arXiv 2605.26934](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.26934)
- Non-decoupling paper (Jan 2026) used Qwen3-0.6B for its SFT↔RL coupling experiments (see KQ3). — [arXiv 2601.07389](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2601.07389)
- CurioSFT and TS-OPSD both use Qwen3-4B-Base for robustness/continued-RL experiments (see KQ2). — [arXiv 2602.02244](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02244v3), [arXiv 2606.00755](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00755)

### Inferences
- Because Qwen3-1.7B/4B were already on-policy-distilled from a 32B/235B teacher on both modes, the released checkpoints are "post-SFT" in the entropy sense; a second distillation-SFT on a foreign teacher's long CoT risks (a) the Li et al. speaking-style shift and (b) further entropy loss before GRPO. The lower-risk cold starts are: own-rollout rejection-sampled SFT in the target format, or a short SFT whose traces were generated by Qwen3-32B/235B (same family, same style) with /no_think or short /think budgets.
- The Qwen3-8B table is the cleanest evidence that "RL on top of distillation" adds +12.6 AIME'24 but does not move pass@64 (90.0→90.0), i.e., it sharpens; on-policy distillation moves pass@64 (93.3). For an agentic task where a teacher cannot be run per-token (tool environments), the practical analogue is rejection-sampled teacher traces or own traces, not logit matching.
- Thinking vs non-thinking: no direct measurement for RL on Qwen3-1.7B/4B was found; the snippets suggest non-thinking mode makes exploration harder (lower entropy, shorter rollouts), while thinking mode on Qwen3 shows early entropy collapse under vanilla GRPO. Treat as hypothesis.

### Gaps
- The exact cold-start set size for Qwen3's flagship stage 1 is not disclosed ("carefully selected subset"); the distillation data sizes for the small models are not disclosed.
- No paper found that ablates /think vs /no_think as the RL starting mode on Qwen3-1.7B or 4B with numbers; the three snippets above are indirect.

## KQ6. Format-learning cold start: is a very small SFT set (hundreds to ~1–2k examples) enough to teach the protocol so RL does the rest?

### Takeaway
Yes, with caveats on *what* the few examples are. DeepSeek-R1 used "thousands" of long-CoT cold-start examples whose purpose was readability/stability, not capability; LIMO reached 63.3% AIME24 with 817 examples (but at 32B); EnvFactory (May 2026) lifts Qwen3-1.7B BFCL-multi-turn 16.75→23.25 with 1,622 SFT trajectories and to 28.38 after RL on 953 more, and reports direct RL without SFT gives "smaller and less stable" gains; SimpleRL-Zoo shows zero-RL works from 0.5B with a loose format reward. Counter-evidence: ToolRL (Apr 2025) finds a 400-example SFT cold start *hurts* Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B/7B vs GRPO from the instruct model (BFCL 1.5B 40.93 vs 46.20; 3B 46.42 vs 52.98; 7B 39.25 vs 58.38) — "SFT initialization leads to memorization and overfitting" — while helping Llama-3.2-3B (whose base could not emit the format).

### Cited Findings
- DeepSeek-R1 (Jan 2025): collected "thousands of cold-start data to fine-tune the DeepSeek-V3-Base as the starting point for RL," created by having annotators rewrite R1-Zero traces into readable form and LLM-expanding them; motivation: R1-Zero's "poor readability and language mixing." — [arXiv HTML 2501.12948](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12948v1)
- LIMO (Feb 2025): Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, 817 examples ("1% of data used in prior approaches"), AIME24 63.3%, MATH500 95.6%; hypothesis: when "domain knowledge has been comprehensively encoded during pre-training, sophisticated reasoning can emerge through minimal but strategically designed demonstrations." (32B — size caveat.) — [arXiv 2502.03387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03387)
- SimpleRL-Zoo (Mar 2025): zero RL (no SFT) across Qwen2.5 0.5B–32B and non-Qwen bases; format reward helps but strict enforcement hurts smaller/weaker models; difficulty-matched queries are the other lever. — [arXiv 2503.18892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18892)
- ToolRL (Apr 2025): Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B/7B-Instruct, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct; three setups — SFT-only on 4K, SFT on 400 then GRPO, GRPO "cold start" directly from the instruct model. BFCL: 1.5B 40.67 / 40.93 / 46.20; 3B 41.97 / 46.42 / 52.98; 7B 36.53 / 39.25 / 58.38 (7B SFT-only value as extracted; looks low, verify); Llama-3.2-3B 44.16 / 42.54 / 44.10. API-Bank: 1.5B 47.07 / 61.31 / 63.15; 3B 50.92 / 62.48 / 67.00; 7B 47.07 / 54.10 / 64.66; Llama 43.89 / 56.78 / 59.13. Bamboogle: 1.5B 23.2 / 38.4 / 44.0; 3B 49.6 / 56.8 / 60.0; 7B 30.4 / 29.6 / 72.0. Statement: "SFT initialization leads to memorization and overfitting, which reduces the impact of GRPO's effectiveness in generalization"; SFT-init models reach higher *training* reward but "empirically generalize worse." Reward: binary format + fine-grained correctness in [−3,3] over tool name / parameter names / values; finer decomposition trained better (contrast Tool-N1 which found binary better: 80.38 vs 76.61 on Live BFCL). Abstract: GRPO gives "a 17% improvement over base models and a 15% gain over SFT models." — [arXiv 2504.13958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13958), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13958)
- Nemotron-Research-Tool-N1 (Apr 2025): binary reward on "format validity and functional correctness of tool invocations" only; pure RL 83.24 ≥ SFT-then-RL 83.17 ≥ Reason-SFT 82.71 on BFCL (7B/14B Qwen2.5-Instruct); 5,518 distilled traces. — [arXiv HTML 2505.00024](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00024)
- EnvFactory (May 2026): Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B; Stage-1 SFT on 1,622 synthesized trajectories, Stage-2 RL on 953 (2,575 total). BFCL multi-turn: 1.7B base 16.75 → SFT 23.25 → SFT+RL 28.38; 4B 33.50 → 44.25 → 48.50; 8B 41.25 → 46.50 → 49.00. τ²-Bench Qwen3-4B 25.25 → 30.13. Up to +15% on BFCLv3, +8.6% on MCP-Atlas. Direct RL without SFT: "gains are smaller and less stable than RL after SFT, indicating that SFT initialization remains important." SFT data synthesised with Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking; training targets are non-thinking Qwen3 models. — [arXiv HTML 2605.18703](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18703)
- CHORD (Aug 2025) tool-use arm: LLaMA3.2-3B-Instruct with only 500 SFT-style expert samples (ToolAce) used as an in-RL auxiliary loss plus 5k RL samples, evaluated on BFCL (per-number not read). — [arXiv HTML 2508.11408](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11408)
- Light-R1 (Mar 2025): the second curriculum-SFT stage is only 3k hard problems, after which GRPO still adds +1.7 AIME24 on a 14B long-CoT model. — [arXiv HTML 2503.10460](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.10460v1)
- "SFT Memorizes, RL Generalizes" (Jan 2025): "SFT stabilizes the model's output format, enabling subsequent RL to achieve its performance gains." — [arXiv 2501.17161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17161)
- Magistral (Jun 2025, 24B): Magistral Small "further includes cold-start data from Magistral Medium" before RL; "RL on text data alone maintains most of the initial checkpoint's capabilities" (abstract-level; no size-ablation numbers read). — [arXiv 2506.10910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10910)

### Inferences
- The deciding variable is whether the untouched policy can already emit a parseable, occasionally-correct trajectory in the target protocol. Qwen2.5-Instruct could for single-turn tool calls (ToolRL: GRPO-only wins at every size), Llama-3.2-3B could not (SFT helped), Qwen3-1.7B could only weakly for multi-turn tool use (EnvFactory base 16.75 and "less stable" direct RL). For DySQL-style multi-turn agent protocols on Qwen3-1.7B, this argues for a small (≈1–2k) format SFT; for Qwen3-4B (base 33.5 on BFCL-MT) direct GRPO is plausible but the EnvFactory authors still found SFT-first more stable.
- Small-SFT cold starts should be *in-distribution for the student* (own or same-family rollouts, short traces) to avoid the ToolRL "memorization" penalty; ToolRL's 400 SFT examples were teacher-style traces and still hurt Qwen.

### Gaps
- No paper found that sweeps cold-start size (e.g., 100 → 2k) on a 1–4B model and reports post-GRPO outcomes; EnvFactory (1,622) and ToolRL (400) are single points with different conclusions.
- The DySQL-relevant SkyRL-SQL 653-example result is out of scope here (text-to-SQL; other researcher).

## KQ7. 2026 papers on cold-start choice for agentic / tool-use RL with small models

### Takeaway
2026 agentic work on ≤8B models converges on "student-centred" cold starts: behaviour-clone a small slice of teacher data (SCoRe uses 20%), then train on student-generated trajectories that a teacher minimally corrects (SCoRe), or on student rollouts verified by the environment (WinDOM EMA, EnvFactory), or with per-step on-policy distillation that is down-weighted when the student has drifted (SOD, Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B). Pure teacher-trajectory SFT consistently loses to these at small scale (SCoRe: BC 43.0 vs GRPO 48.2 vs SCoRe-RL 52.3 avg over 12 benchmarks; deep search Qwen3-8B 22.8 / 22.2 / 30.5).

### Cited Findings
- SCoRe, "Student-Centered Distillation Narrows the Agentic Gap Between Small and Large LLMs" (Sep 2025, v3 Jul 2026). Students Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (primary), Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct; teacher Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct; 12 benchmarks (AIME24/25, MATH500, OlymMath; HotpotQA, 2Wiki, Musique, Bamboogle; GAIA, WebWalker, HLE, xBench). Recipe: (1) cold-start BC on 20% high-quality teacher trajectories; (2) Mentored Problem-Solving — student generates, teacher corrects only the first error; (3) correction-based SFT on those student-generated/teacher-edited traces; (4) short-horizon RL from verified prefixes with key-step rewards. Why small students fail on teacher data: a "reasoning ability gap: smaller models often fail to reproduce the teacher's logical decomposition" and a "knowledge capability gap." Results: SCoRe-RL 52.3 avg vs BC 43.0 vs GRPO 48.2; deep search Qwen3-8B 30.5 vs BC 22.8 vs GRPO 22.2; error growth reduced from O(H²) to O(H). Abstract: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct reaches tool-use performance "comparable to that of a 72B teacher, outperforming both Behavior Cloning and GRPO by a large margin." — [arXiv HTML 2509.14257](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14257v3)
- SOD (May 2026): Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B students, Qwen3-4B teacher, tool-integrated reasoning; wrong tool calls corrupt later teacher supervision; per-step divergence-weighted OPD; up to 20.86% relative over best baseline (SFT, GRPO, OPD, self-distillation); 0.6B 26.13% avg AIME25. — [arXiv HTML 2605.07725](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07725v1)
- WinDOM (Jun 2026): Qwen3.5-2B GUI-grounding agent; single rejection-sampling cold start with EMA-of-self or frozen 4B same-family teacher; under-saturated cold start → +5.4 OOD-mean after identical GRPO; EMA teacher within 1.1 of the 4B teacher. — [arXiv HTML 2606.25964](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25964v1)
- EnvFactory (May 2026): Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B; SFT 1,622 → RL 953 trajectories; BFCL-MT 1.7B 16.75→23.25→28.38, 4B 33.50→44.25→48.50; direct RL "smaller and less stable." — [arXiv HTML 2605.18703](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18703)
- DGPO (v4 Apr 2026): 0.5B agentic-RAG student; KD cold start then RL with teacher-KL only on wrong answers; 0.329 vs PPO 0.238 vs KD 0.298 vs teacher 0.353. — [arXiv HTML 2508.20324](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20324)
- AgenticQwen (Apr 2026): "small agentic language models" = AgenticQwen-8B and 30B-A3B; ~100K total training data with GRPO; TAU-2 avg 47.4 (8B) vs Qwen3-8B 23.8; BFCL-V4 50.2 (30B-A3B) vs 36.0; no SFT-vs-RL ablation in the pages read; no 1.7B/4B variants. — [arXiv HTML 2604.21590](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21590v1)
- PACT (Jun 2026) and SEED (Jul 2026): expert traces / self-written skills used only inside RL as privileged or hindsight context, never at inference; sizes/numbers not on abstract pages (see KQ4). — [arXiv 2606.16215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16215), [arXiv 2607.14777](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.14777)
- "On Effectiveness and Efficiency of Agentic Tool-calling and RL Training" (May 2026, ICML 2026): multi-turn tool-calling leaderboards are "highly sensitive to seemingly minor" choices (seeds, system prompts); proposes two RL accelerations (skip prompts with no learning signal; cheaper policy updates); no SFT-vs-RL numbers on abstract page. — [arXiv 2606.00135](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.00135)
- SDFT (Jan 2026) tool-use task: on-policy self-distillation > offline teacher distillation > SFT, and SDFT "can serve as an effective initialization for subsequent RL fine-tuning." — [arXiv HTML 2601.19897](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19897v1)
- Tool-N1 (Apr 2025) and ToolRL (Apr 2025): see KQ6 — on Qwen2.5-Instruct single-turn tool calling, RL without SFT matches or beats SFT→RL at 1.5B–14B. — [arXiv 2505.00024](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00024), [arXiv 2504.13958](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13958)

### Inferences
- The 2026 agentic consensus for ≤4B students: (1) a *small* BC/format cold start (SCoRe 20% of teacher data; EnvFactory 1.6k); (2) the bulk of imitation data should be *student-generated* (teacher-corrected or environment-verified), because teacher trajectories exceed the student's expressible policy and multi-turn errors make later teacher supervision unreliable (SOD, SCoRe); (3) stop the cold start before saturation (WinDOM) so GRPO retains group variance; (4) if a same-family stronger model exists (Qwen3-32B for Qwen3-1.7B/4B), it is the preferred source for both rejection-sampled traces and per-step OPD.
- For a 1.7B policy with a low base success rate on hard tasks, DGPO/SCoRe-style "teacher only when wrong" RL regularisation is the documented way to avoid the zero-reward cliff without over-imitating.

### Gaps
- No 2026 paper found that reports a cold-start ablation on Qwen3-1.7B *and* Qwen3-4B for multi-turn tool use with the same RL recipe other than EnvFactory (which gives base/SFT/SFT+RL but only a qualitative statement on RL-without-SFT).
- No agentic paper found that compares own-rollout RFT against teacher-trajectory SFT as the cold start on a text-only multi-turn environment at ≤4B; WinDOM (GUI, 2B VLM) is the nearest.
